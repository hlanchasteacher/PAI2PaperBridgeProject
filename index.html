Structural Engineering Challenge

Welcome, engineers!

In universities and engineering schools around the world, students face challenges with limited resources. This forces them to develop their problem-solving skills and understand how physics work.

Your new mission is to span a gap by building a bridge.

⚠️ The Catch:

Test 1: Tension (Pulling)

Goal: Find out how much pulling force the material can take before it rips. This is important for suspension cables.

Instructions:

  1. Cut a strip of your material exactly 3 cm wide and 15 cm long.
  2. Tape the top edge of the strip securely to the edge of your desk.
  3. Tape a small plastic cup to the bottom edge of the strip.
  4. Gently place weights (coins or small lab weights) into the cup, one by one.

📝 What to record:

Count the exact number of weights the paper held just before it broke. Write this number down in your draft table!

📚

Test 2: Compression (Squashing)

Goal: Find out how much weight the material can support from above before it crushes. This is important for bridge pillars.

Instructions:

  1. Roll a piece of your material to create a hollow cylinder (about 10 cm tall).
  2. Use a single piece of tape to keep the cylinder closed.
  3. Stand the cylinder upright on a flat table.
  4. Carefully place books on top of the cylinder, one by one. Make sure you balance them perfectly in the center!

📝 What to record:

Write down the exact number of books it supported before the cylinder crumpled or collapsed.

📏

Test 3: Folding & Adhesion

Goal: Check if the material keeps a shape when folded, and if liquid glue actually works on it.

Instructions:

  1. The Fold Test: Fold a piece of the material in half. Press the crease hard with your fingernail. Let it go. Does it open back up like a spring, or does it stay perfectly folded?
  2. The Glue Test: Put a small drop of school glue between two pieces of the material. Wait exactly 3 minutes, then try to pull them apart.

📝 What to record:

Rate the folding from 1 to 5. Also, write down if the glue held them together tightly, or if they easily slipped apart.

📐

Test 4: The Geometry Test

Goal: Materials are weak on their own, but geometry makes them strong. Find out which shape you should use to build the framework of your bridge.

Instructions:

  1. Roll 7 small pieces of normal printer paper into thin, tight tubes. Tape them so they don't unroll.
  2. Shape A (Square): Tape 4 of the tubes together at the corners to make a flat square.
  3. Shape B (Triangle): Tape the other 3 tubes together at the corners to make a flat triangle.
  4. Stand both shapes up on the table. Gently push the top corner of the Square side-to-side. Then, do the same to the top corner of the Triangle.

📝 What to record:

What happened to the Square? Did it keep its shape? What happened to the Triangle? Write down which shape is stronger against moving forces.
